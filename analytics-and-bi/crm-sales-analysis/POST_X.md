1/6 Two B2B sales pods in the same territory have almost identical win rates (62.20% vs 62.98%). Yet Pod A books $2.25M and Pod B books $1.09M.

I audited 8,800 CRM pipeline records ($10.01M closed-won across 85 accounts & 35 reps) to find out why. 🧵👇

2/6 Funnel baseline across 8,800 opportunities:
• Won: 4,238 deals ($10,005,534 revenue | $2,360.91 mean ASP | $1,117 median ASP)
• Lost: 2,473 deals
• Active Engaging: 1,589 | Prospecting: 500
• Resolved Win Rate: 63.15% | Mean Sales Cycle: 51.8 days (median 57.0 days).

3/6 Finding #1 — The Low-Ticket Activity Trap:
The $55 `MG Special` SKU accounts for 18.22% of all resolved rep opportunities (1,223 deals, 793 wins, 64.84% win rate) — but generates only $43,768 (0.44% of total revenue!).

4/6 Finding #2 — Where the money actually comes from:
• `GTXPro` ($4,821 list): 729 wins → $3,510,578 (35.09% of revenue)
• `GTX Plus Pro` ($5,482 list): 479 wins → $2,629,651 (26.28% of revenue)
• `MG Advanced` ($3,393 list): 654 wins → $2,216,387 (22.15% of revenue)
Three SKUs generate 83.52% of all bookings.

5/6 Finding #3 — Price realization is tight:
Realized average close price tracks within ±1.5% of list price across all 7 products. Reps aren't losing revenue to discounting—they're losing capacity to $55 and $550 orders.

6/6 Recommendation: Route `MG Special` ($55) and `GTX Basic` ($550) to self-serve checkout to free up 39.6% of AE capacity for $3.4k–$26.7k enterprise hardware deals. Full case study in `analytics-and-bi/crm-sales-analysis`.
