Before you score 1,000 new leads with a customer lifetime value (LTV) model, inspect your relational joins—dirty master data will silently corrupt your top decile.

In my KPMG Sprocket Central & PwC BI Analytics project (`20,000` retail cycling transactions joined to `4,000` demographic and `3,999` address records across Python, R, Power BI, and Tableau), fixing the relational schema unlocked three margin truths across **\$21.95M in approved revenue** and **\$10.83M in gross profit**:

1. **Volume ≠ Profit (`WeareA2B` vs `Solex`):** While `Solex` led order volume (`4,211` approved orders, `\$4.45M` revenue at `53.71%` margin), **`WeareA2B` generated the #1 profit pool (\$2.73M, 25.20% of company profit)** on 22% fewer orders (`3,265`) because of its **66.16% gross margin**—more than double `Norco Bicycles` (`32.62%` margin).
2. **Product-Line Margin Asymmetry:** The `Standard` line drives **74.45% of total gross profit (\$8.06M)**, while `Touring` bikes command the highest unit profitability at **67.48% gross margin** (`\$1.34M` profit on `\$1.98M` revenue).
3. **Where to Target the 1,000 New Leads:** `Mass Customer` households account for **50.16% of total gross profit (\$5.43M)** and **New South Wales (NSW)** accounts for **53.04% (\$5.74M)**—making mid-career Mass Market buyers in NSW/VIC the highest-expected-value acquisition cohort.
